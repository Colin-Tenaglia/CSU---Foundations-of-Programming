Disclosure

What this document is, and what it is not

This is a working reference that also runs as a training sequence. It explains how an individual return is built and how Drake Tax is navigated. Read straight through, it teaches. Used by section, it answers a question mid-return. It is written for anyone preparing a Form 1040 — someone learning the work, someone experienced checking a detail, or someone preparing their own return.

It is not tax advice for any particular situation, and it is not a substitute for professional judgment. A return with anything unusual in it — a business, a rental, a K-1, a foreign account, a death in the family, a disputed dependent — deserves a preparer, and this document does not change that.

It is not authority. The Golden Notes, plain-English explanations, and summaries here are deliberate teaching simplifications. Real rules carry exceptions, limitations, phaseouts, and interactions that a one-sentence takeaway cannot hold. When preparing an actual return, the governing sources are the Internal Revenue Code and regulations, IRS forms and instructions, IRS publications, and for Wisconsin the Wisconsin Statutes and Department of Revenue instructions. Every lesson links to those sources for exactly this reason. Where this document and a primary source disagree, the primary source wins and this document is wrong.

It goes stale. Content reflects tax year 2025 and information available as of preparation. Tax law changes, Drake screen codes change between program years, and Wisconsin's conformity date may move. Amounts are deliberately not written into the lessons — they live in the Figures & Lines Sheet below, which is the one place that gets updated each year.

Verification status is stated honestly throughout. Federal Drake screen codes were confirmed against Drake's Knowledge Base. Wisconsin forms were confirmed against Drake KB 20050 and Wisconsin Department of Revenue forms. Anything still unconfirmed is marked as such rather than presented as settled. Confirm against your own installed program year before relying on any screen reference.

Learn on practice returns. Use the practice and test returns that ship with Drake Tax rather than live data. If you are working inside a firm, IRC §7216 governs the use and disclosure of taxpayer information and applies to someone learning the work exactly as it applies to everyone else.

Scope

This covers the individual income tax return, Form 1040, from intake through filing. Everything a personal return can contain is either here or named in the table below as not yet written. Entity returns — 1065, 1120-S, 1120, 1041, 709 — are out of scope except where a Schedule K-1 feeds the individual return.

Finishing the return is part of the work, not an afterthought: running View, clearing every EF message, checking the bank details, setting up the payment or the vouchers, the signature, and transmission. Lesson 12 in Part 1 covers all of it.

One role note, which is about firms and not about this document. Where a return is prepared for compensation inside a firm, the preparer who signs it owns the e-file authorization, the PIN screen, the EF selections, and the transmission, and that person carries the Circular 230 and §6695 exposure. If you are learning inside a firm, expect to hand off at that line. If you are preparing your own return, there is no handoff and the whole sequence is yours. Either way the return has to be finished correctly before anyone touches the transmit button, which is why the finishing steps are taught rather than skipped.

Where to verify a figure

Every amount in the sheet above is checkable in under a minute, and you should check rather than trust — including trusting this document. Where this sheet and a primary source disagree, the source wins and this sheet is wrong. Fix it here, once, so the next person is not caught by it.

The one thing to understand about federal amounts

Almost every inflation-adjusted federal figure for a year is published in a single revenue procedure, released the autumn before that tax year. Rev. Proc. 2024-40 carries the 2025 amounts. Rev. Proc. 2025-32 carries the 2026 amounts. Standard deduction, bracket thresholds, the qualifying relative gross income limit, the student loan interest phaseout, the EITC tables, the adoption credit, the foreign earned income exclusion — one document, all of it. Retirement and health accounts are the exception and come out separately.

Drake

Any knowledge base article is reachable as kb.drakesoftware.com/kb/Drake-Tax/<number>.htm, so an article number is a complete reference. Start at the Federal 1040 screen list, KB 20051 or the Wisconsin form availability list, KB 20050. Article numbers are stable year to year; the content inside them is revised, so read against your installed program.

For anything about a screen's layout, F1 inside that screen beats every link on this page, because it answers for the program year actually installed. No document can do that, including this one.

Sanity check before you rely on a number. Confirm the source is for the right tax year — a 2026 figure on a 2025 return is the most common way this goes wrong, and the two sit next to each other in search results. Then confirm the figure is the one you think it is: caps, floors and phaseout start points are different things, and several items have separate amounts by filing status.
